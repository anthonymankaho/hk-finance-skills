# General Learning (Hong Kong Finance)

## Purpose

Teach the requested Hong Kong finance topic so the learner can apply it. Prefer clear explanation over procedure checklists. When the question is mainly operational (prepare a journal, compute ECL, review a report), prefer the matching topic skill and use this skill only for the teaching layer.

## Teaching approach

1. **Clarify the learning goal** — Concept overview, exam-style explanation, worked example, or comparison with IFRS/IAS or another jurisdiction? If the goal is unclear, ask one focused question.
2. **Start from the learner's level** — Give a short plain-language definition first. Add technical terms, standard references, and exceptions only after the core idea is clear.
3. **Hong Kong first** — Anchor on HKFRS/HKAS, the Inland Revenue Ordinance, or HKICPA/IRD guidance as relevant. Mention IFRS/IAS equivalents when they help, and note where Hong Kong practice differs.
4. **Show structure before detail** — Outline the topic (purpose → recognition/measurement or charging rules → presentation/disclosure or filing → common pitfalls), then expand only the parts the user asked about.
5. **Use a worked example when useful** — Prefer simple HKD figures, named accounts, and one entity. State assumptions. Do not invent company-specific facts as if they were real.
6. **Point to the next skill** — When the learner is ready to perform the task, name the practical next step and rely on the matching topic skill for the procedure.

## Explanation pattern

For most topics, cover in this order unless the user asks for a narrower focus:

1. **What it is** — One or two sentences.
2. **Why it matters** — Reporting, tax, control, or decision use.
3. **Key rules** — Recognition, measurement, classification, or charging basis under current Hong Kong sources.
4. **How it appears** — Statements, notes, tax computation, or process artefacts.
5. **Common mistakes** — Frequent mix-ups, cut-off errors, disallowable items, or disclosure gaps.
6. **Check sources** — Remind the learner to verify effective dates and current HKICPA/IRD material before relying on the explanation for filing or audit conclusions.

## Scope boundaries

- Teach and illustrate; do not approve accounting judgments, file returns, or change records.
- Distinguish general teaching examples from the learner's real company data. When company facts are missing, say what is needed rather than filling gaps with invented figures.
- If the topic depends on election, early application, transitional relief, or a period before an effective date (for example HKFRS 18), say so before treating the rule as currently applicable.
- Keep answers proportional: a short definition for a quick question; a structured lesson for "teach me" or "walk me through" requests.
- Use the user's language. For Chinese, reply in Traditional Chinese.
